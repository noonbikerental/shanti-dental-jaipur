A root canal treatment (RCT) saves a tooth whose inner pulp has become infected or badly inflamed. Many people delay treatment because they fear it will be painful, but modern root canal treatment is done under local anaesthesia and is usually no more uncomfortable than a regular filling.

The earlier an infection is treated, the better the chances of saving your natural tooth. Here are seven common signs that you should get your tooth checked.

## 1. Lasting tooth pain

Pain that keeps coming back, or a deep ache that doesn't go away, can mean the nerve inside the tooth is affected. The pain may also spread to your jaw, ear or face.

## 2. Sensitivity to hot or cold that lingers

A quick twinge from cold water is common. But if the sensitivity stays for several seconds or minutes after you stop eating or drinking, it can be a sign of nerve damage.

## 3. Pain while chewing or biting

If it hurts when you bite down or press on a tooth, the tissue around the root may be inflamed or infected.

## 4. Swollen or tender gums

Swelling near one tooth, or a small pimple-like bump on the gum, can point to an infection at the root.

## 5. A darkened tooth

A tooth that turns grey or darker than the teeth around it may have damaged pulp inside.

## 6. A cracked or chipped tooth

Cracks and chips let bacteria reach the inner part of the tooth, which can lead to infection over time.

## 7. A deep cavity

When decay goes deep into the tooth, it can reach the pulp. A filling may no longer be enough at that stage.

## What to do next

Not every toothache means you need a root canal. Only a dentist can confirm it after an examination and a dental X-ray. If you notice any of these signs, don't wait for the pain to get worse. Early treatment is simpler, faster and more likely to save your natural tooth.
